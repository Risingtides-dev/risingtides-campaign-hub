# 2. Who Does What

## Jake

Jake is the founder and main operator of Rising Tides. He's the one who:

- Negotiates and closes deals with record labels
- Decides which creators to book for each campaign
- Reviews and approves creator booking suggestions in the Slack Inbox
- Oversees payments to creators
- Makes the final call on anything that looks wrong

Campaign Hub was built for Jake first — it mirrors the way he already thought about campaigns, just without the spreadsheets.

## The Rising Tides team

Beyond Jake, the team includes account managers and operations staff who use Campaign Hub to monitor active campaigns, update creator information, and track payments. The team is small, which is why Campaign Hub is an internal tool with no login required — everyone who has the link is trusted.

## Creators

Creators are the TikTok or Instagram users who make and post the actual videos. They are **independent people**, not employees of Rising Tides. Rising Tides books them (agrees on a price and number of posts), they post, and Rising Tides pays them — usually through PayPal.

What a creator typically agrees to do:
- Post a set number of videos (e.g. "3 posts") using a specific sound
- Post within a certain window (e.g. during the campaign period)
- Use the official TikTok/Instagram sound link the label provides

What the team tracks for each creator in Campaign Hub:
- Their TikTok or Instagram username
- How many posts they owe
- How many they've delivered
- Their rate (total amount they'll be paid)
- Whether they've been paid yet, and through which PayPal address

## The Labels / Clients

The labels are the record labels (and sometimes individual artists or management teams) who hire Rising Tides. They are the paying clients. In Campaign Hub's terminology, "label" and "client" mean the same thing.

Examples: Warner Music Group, Atlantic Records, Republic Records, and their affiliates.

The label's relationship with Rising Tides is tracked in **Notion** (the CRM), not in Campaign Hub. Campaign Hub only stores what it needs to run the campaign — the artist name, the song, the budget, and a reference back to the Notion booking record.

## What is "UGC"?

UGC stands for **User Generated Content**. In this context, it means videos made by real, ordinary social media users — not by the label's official marketing team.

Why do labels pay for it? Because content from real creators performs better than polished label ads. When a TikTok user's followers see them dancing to a song, it feels authentic. If that happens across dozens of creators at once, the song starts trending organically — even though Rising Tides carefully orchestrated it behind the scenes.

A UGC campaign on TikTok might involve 20 creators each posting 2–3 videos using the same sound. That's 40–60 pieces of content hitting at roughly the same time, driving the song into TikTok's algorithm.
