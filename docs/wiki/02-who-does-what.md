## Creators

Creators are the TikTok and Instagram users who make and post the actual videos. They are **independent people, not employees of Rising Tides**. Rising Tides books them (agrees on a price and a number of posts), they post on their own schedule, and Rising Tides pays them — usually through PayPal.

### What a creator typically agrees to

- Post a set number of videos (e.g. "3 posts") using a specific sound
- Post within the campaign's window
- Use the official TikTok or Instagram sound the label provides

### What Campaign Hub tracks for each creator

- TikTok or Instagram username
- How many posts they owe
- How many they've delivered
- Their rate (the total amount they'll be paid)
- Their PayPal email
- Whether they've been paid yet

The same creator often appears across many campaigns over time. The **Creator Database** screen rolls up every campaign a creator has done, every post they've delivered, and how much they've been paid in total. That history is one of the main reasons Campaign Hub exists.

## Labels (the clients)

The labels are the record companies (and sometimes individual artists or management teams) who hire Rising Tides. They are the paying clients.

In Campaign Hub's world, "label" and "client" mean the same thing.

**Examples:** Warner Music Group, Atlantic Records, Republic Records, and their affiliates.

The label's relationship with Rising Tides — contract terms, billing, contact info — is tracked in **Notion** (the CRM), not in Campaign Hub. Campaign Hub only stores what it needs to run the campaign: the artist name, the song, the budget Rising Tides is allocating to creators, and a reference back to the Notion booking.

## What is "UGC"?

UGC stands for **User-Generated Content**. In this context, it means videos made by real, ordinary social media users — not by the label's official marketing team.

Why do labels pay for it? Because content from real creators performs better than polished label ads. When a TikTok user's followers see them dancing to a song, it feels authentic. If that happens across dozens of creators at once, the song starts trending organically — even though Rising Tides carefully orchestrated it behind the scenes.

A typical TikTok UGC campaign might involve 20 creators each posting 2–3 videos using the same sound. That's 40–60 pieces of content hitting at roughly the same time, driving the song into TikTok's algorithm.

Creators disclose that the post is paid (per FTC rules), but the creative — the angle, the framing, the dance, the joke — is theirs. That's what makes UGC feel different from an ad.
